FAQ

Questions people ask about Parley

Is Parley really free?

Yes. Live translation, all 28 languages, spoken playback and your last five saved conversations are free with no account. If Parley is useful you can chip in whatever you want, one time, and get unlimited history, recordings kept until you delete them, and transcript export.

Do I need to download an app?

No. Parley runs in Chrome, Edge or Safari. You can add it to your home screen from the browser and it then opens full screen with its own icon, like any app. On Android and desktop Chrome the Install button does this for you; on iPhone tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

How does Parley know which language is being spoken?

It reads the first two words. A small on-device list of the most common words in each language usually settles it instantly; for languages with different alphabets the script alone is enough; otherwise the translation engine identifies the language. If Parley guesses wrong, it switches and keeps what it heard, and either person can tap their language chip to force it.

Is there a delay?

The translation starts appearing while the sentence is still being said and is finalized about a second after you stop talking. The engine switches languages during that silence so the other person's reply is heard correctly.

Where do my conversations go?

They stay on your device. Transcripts and audio are stored in your browser's local database. There is no Parley account and no Parley server that sees your conversation. Speech recognition is done by your browser's engine and translation by public translation engines, or by your own LibreTranslate server if you set one up.

Which languages does Parley support?

28 at the moment: English, Spanish, Portuguese, Chinese, Hindi, Thai, Korean, Japanese, French, German, Italian, Arabic, Russian, Indonesian, Vietnamese, Turkish, Dutch, Polish, Filipino, Swahili, Bengali, Urdu, Hebrew, Greek, Swedish, Ukrainian, Malay and Persian.

Does Parley work offline?

Yes, on phones and computers with GPU support in the browser (recent iPhones, most Android phones from the last few years, and any Mac or PC). Turn on Offline mode on the start screen: Parley downloads the open-source Whisper speech model once (about 290 MB), and from then on it transcribes on the device with no signal. Translation offline uses the browser's built-in translator where it exists (Chrome on Android and desktop); elsewhere it falls back to the online engines when a connection is available. Offline mode delivers words in short bursts after each pause rather than live, word by word.

Does Parley work on a laptop?

Yes, the same link works on macOS, Windows and Linux in Chrome or Edge, and in Safari on a Mac. It is handy for a desk conversation or a video call where the other person is in the room with you.

Can it speak the translation out loud?

Yes. Turn on Read translations out loud. Voices come from Kokoro, an open-source neural text-to-speech model that downloads once and runs on your device.

How is Parley different from Google Translate or Apple Translate?

Nothing to install, no account, the whole screen is the conversation, conversations replay with audio, and you can use your own translation server. The native apps cover more languages and can work fully offline. See the full comparison page.

What do supporters get?

Unlimited conversation history instead of the last five, recordings kept until you delete them, transcript export, and a Keep setting to auto-trim history to any number you choose. It is pay what you want, one time, and the same email unlocks any other device.

Is Parley open source?

The source is on GitHub. Underneath, Parley relies on open projects and credits them: Kokoro for voices, LibreTranslate and Argos Translate for self-hosted translation, and the ideas in Handy and whisper.cpp.

Who makes Parley?

Parley is made by 0xROSSI Digital, an independent studio run by Frankie Rossi.
