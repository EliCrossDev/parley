How it works

Live conversation translation, explained simply.

Parley is a web app for two people who do not share a language and are standing in the same place. There is no call, no account and nothing to download from an app store. Here is what happens underneath.

1. Two languages, one screen

You pick the two languages in the room, for example English and Spanish. It does not matter who is who. Put the phone between you and tap Start. Light bubbles are one language, dark bubbles are the other, large enough to read across a table.

2. Speech is recognized by your browser

Parley uses the speech recognition built into Chrome, Edge and Safari. Nothing is uploaded to a Parley server, because there is no Parley server in the loop: the browser hears you and hands Parley the words as you say them, with interim results arriving several times a second.

3. Parley decides who is talking from the first two words

The browser's engine can only listen in one language at a time, so Parley has to know which language is being spoken as early as possible. It checks the first two words against a tiny on-device list of the most common words in each language (articles, pronouns, greetings). For languages that use different alphabets it looks at the script instead. When that is not enough, it asks the translation engine to identify the language. If the guess was wrong, Parley switches the recognizer and keeps the words it already heard. Either person can also tap their language chip to force it.

4. Translation appears as the sentence is still being said

Every fraction of a second, the in-progress sentence is translated and shown in the big text of the bubble while the raw words stay in the small line above it. When the room goes quiet, Parley commits the line, translates the final version, and switches the engine to the other language during that silence, so the reply is heard in the right language.

5. Optional voice

Turn on "Read translations out loud" and Parley speaks the translation with Kokoro, an open-source neural voice model that downloads to your device once and then runs offline. There is no per-use cost, which is part of why Parley is free.

6. Everything stays on your device

Transcripts and audio recordings are stored in your browser's own database, grouped by day, and can be replayed bubble by bubble. The free tier keeps your last five conversations; supporters keep all of them and can export transcripts.
