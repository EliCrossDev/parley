Why we built a live conversation translator that runs in the browser

2026-10-20 · by Frankie Rossi, 0xROSSI Digital

Most translation apps were designed around a text box. Parley was designed around two people, a table, and a phone lying between them.

The moment we wanted to fix

You are at a market, a clinic, a landlord's office, a dinner. The other person does not speak your language. Both of you reach for a phone, open an app, tap through screens, hold it up, wait. The conversation that was supposed to happen has become a demo of an app.

We wanted the opposite: one link, one screen, both of you looking at the same bubbles, no account, nothing to set up beyond picking two languages. The whole app is the conversation.

Why the browser

Chrome and Safari already ship excellent speech recognition, and open translation engines are a request away. By building Parley as a web app we skip the store, work on every phone and laptop with one link, and keep your words off our servers because there are no servers of ours in the loop. It also means Parley can be installed to the home screen and run full screen, like an app, in two taps.

What it cost us

Browser speech engines only listen in one language at a time. A native app with its own speech model can identify the language from the sound; we cannot. So we built a different approach: decide from the first two words, switch during silences, never mid-word. We wrote about it in How Parley knows which language is being spoken.

What it made possible

Free for everyone, because nothing meters. Private by construction. Replayable conversations, with audio, on your device. And a translation engine you can swap for your own server when you need unlimited, private use. Try it at getparley.vercel.app/app.
