Parley vs Google Translate vs Apple Translate

All three can translate a conversation between two people in the same room. They differ in where they run, what they keep, and how they decide who is speaking. This page is written by the Parley team, so read it with that in mind; where we are unsure how a competitor behaves today, we say so.

When Parley is the better pick

You want something that opens instantly on any phone or laptop with nothing to install, you care that no account is involved, you want to replay the conversation later with the audio, or you want to point the translator at your own server for private, unlimited use. Parley's whole screen is the conversation, with nothing else competing for attention.

When the big apps are the better pick

You need one of the many languages Parley does not cover yet, you want fully offline translation with downloaded language packs, or you already live inside the Google or Apple ecosystem and want history synced to your account. Google Translate also handles text in photos and documents, which Parley does not try to do.

How the detection actually differs

The browser speech engines Parley relies on can only listen in one language per session. Parley works around that by guessing from the first two words and switching during silences, which we explain in How it works. The native apps run their own speech models and identify the language from the audio itself. In practice both approaches feel immediate; the native apps have the edge when both languages sound alike and the first words are ambiguous, and Parley has the edge in getting started and in keeping your words off a company server.
